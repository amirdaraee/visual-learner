Inside a cell

A cell is the smallest living unit. Inside its membrane, compartments called organelles each do one job: the nucleus stores the DNA, ribosomes build proteins, mitochondria release energy, and so on. They work together, as the protein's route in step 3 shows.

What is simplified

The model is schematic and not to scale. A real cell is far more crowded: it has millions of ribosomes, hundreds to thousands of mitochondria in many cell types, and its organelles change shape and move all the time. Sizes, numbers and positions here are chosen to be readable. Real cells come in many shapes; this one is a generic round animal cell and a generic leaf-like plant cell. Not shown: peroxisomes, endosomes, the many small vesicles moving at any moment, and the fact that the ER and the nuclear envelope are one connected membrane.

Sources

Volumes of a liver cell: Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell, chapter 12. Number of cells in the body: Sender, Fuchs and Milo, PLoS Biology (2016). Sizes are typical textbook values and vary between cell types.
