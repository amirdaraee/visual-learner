Find the variant

A patient has a rare disease and nobody knows why. Exome sequencing reads the protein-coding exons of all ~20,000 genes, finds every place the patient differs from the reference genome, and narrows about 25,000 differences to the one that explains the illness. About 25–40% of rare-disease patients get a diagnosis this way.

1 · Genome

The genome has 3.1 billion bases; the exons add up to roughly 40 million (~1.5%). Early studies put about 85% of known large-effect disease variants in exons, partly because exons were what people could read. The exome gets you ~100× depth for the price of a ~30× genome.

2 · Capture

DNA is sheared to 150–300 bases and tagged with adapters. Biotin-labelled baits tile every exon and bind their matching fragments. Streptavidin beads hold the baits, a magnet holds the beads, and everything else is washed away. Captured fragments are amplified and sequenced as short paired reads.

3 · Reads

Reads are aligned to the reference (BWA-MEM), duplicates are marked, and a caller (GATK, DeepVariant) looks for sites where reads disagree with the reference. About half the reads is heterozygous, nearly all is homozygous, and a lone mismatch is usually sequencing noise. The result is a VCF file.

4 · Filter

Quality (depth ≥ 20×, genotype quality ≥ 30), rarity (gnomAD frequency below ~0.1%, since common variants cannot cause rare disease), impact (stop-gain, frameshift, splice, damaging missense), inheritance and phenotype match (HPO terms against OMIM, Orphanet, ClinGen; Exomiser ranks the rest). Counts are typical, not exact.

5 · Family

Sequencing both parents (a trio) shows whether a variant is de novo, inherited, homozygous in a recessive family, or one of two different hits in the same gene (compound heterozygous). Trios push yield toward the top of the 25–40% range.

6 · Classify

The ACMG/AMP guidelines weigh evidence codes (very strong, strong, moderate, supporting) into five tiers: pathogenic, likely pathogenic, uncertain significance (VUS), likely benign, benign. Pathogenic and likely pathogenic become a diagnosis, ideally confirmed by Sanger sequencing and family segregation. The rules here are simplified.

7 · Limits

The exome misses repeat expansions (Huntington, Fragile X), many structural variants, deep intronic and regulatory changes, and low-level mosaicism. Whole-genome sequencing recovers some of these, and reanalysing exome data every year or two often adds roughly another 10% of diagnoses.
