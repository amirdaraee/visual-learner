Levers and pulleys

A lever balances when effort × effort arm = load × load arm, measured from the fulcrum. A pulley system with n rope sections holding the load needs an effort of the load ÷ n. In both, the ratio by which the force shrinks is the mechanical advantage, and you pay for it in distance: the work in (force × distance) equals the work out.

What is simplified

These are ideal machines: no friction, a weightless plank and weightless pulleys. Real ones need somewhat more force. The load is 300 N, about the weight of a 30 kg mass on Earth (30 kg × 9.8 N/kg ≈ 294 N). The lever is drawn about 2 m long, so 1 scene unit is about 25 cm; on the pulley frame 1 unit of rope is about 50 cm. The forearm example uses typical distances, which vary from person to person.
