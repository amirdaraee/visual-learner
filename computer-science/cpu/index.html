How a real CPU works

The CPU (central processing unit) follows instructions: it fetches one from memory, works out what it asks, does it, and repeats. This page covers five parts of a real one: the floor plan of the chip, one instruction in the real RISC-V RV32I instruction set, the adder that an add uses, the pipeline that overlaps instructions, and the caches that hide slow memory.

Everything is simplified. Real chips have far more instructions, longer pipelines and bigger caches, and numbers on this page are typical values that vary by product.
