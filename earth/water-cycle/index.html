The water cycle

The Sun's heat evaporates water, mostly from the sea. The vapour rises, cools and condenses into cloud droplets. The droplets grow and fall as rain or snow, and the water returns to the sea by rivers (runoff) or slowly through the ground (groundwater).

What is simplified

The scene is schematic. The mountain, the sea and the cloud level are not to scale, and everything happens in seconds instead of days to millennia. Each dot stands for a parcel of water; the counts on the card are counts of dots, not real amounts. The cloud level is fixed here; in real weather it depends on how warm and moist the air is. Evaporation follows how much vapour air can hold at the sea's temperature, which roughly doubles every 10 °C. Water given off by plants, lakes, snow and ice, and the slow melting of glaciers, are left out.
