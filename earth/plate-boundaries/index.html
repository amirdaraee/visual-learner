Plate boundaries

Earth's rigid outer shell is broken into tectonic plates that move a few centimetres a year over the slowly flowing mantle. Where two plates pull apart, magma builds new sea floor at a ridge. Where they push together, an ocean plate sinks under another plate, or two continents crumple into mountains. Where they slide past each other, stress builds on the fault until it slips in an earthquake.

What is simplified

The slices are schematic: heights and thicknesses are greatly exaggerated next to distances, and millions of years pass in seconds. Across the slice, 1 unit stands for roughly 100 km. The mantle flow in step 1 is a simple picture of slow convection, not a measured pattern. In step 4 the plates move at ~3.5 cm a year and the fault slips after 4 to 6 m of stored bending, roughly the amounts released in large San Andreas earthquakes; real faults are far less regular.
