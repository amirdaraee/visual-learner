The Hohmann transfer

To move a spacecraft from a low circular orbit to a higher one, the cheapest two-burn path is an ellipse that touches both circles. Burn forward once to stretch the orbit out, coast half an ellipse, then burn forward again to round it off. This page works through the numbers for an Earth-sized planet.

What the model leaves out

Burns are instant (impulsive). Both orbits are circular and lie in one plane. Gravity comes from one point mass, with no atmosphere, no Moon or Sun, no oblateness, no plane change and no fuel use beyond Δv. Real transfers add all of these. Time is scaled so a transfer takes seconds, and the planet’s continents are invented. Orbit sizes are drawn to scale; the spacecraft, arrows and labels are not.

Constants and sources

Earth’s gravitational parameter μ ~398,600 km³/s² and mean radius ~6,378 km. Formulas are the standard two-body results found in orbital mechanics texts (for example Bate, Mueller and White, Fundamentals of Astrodynamics; Vallado, Fundamentals of Astrodynamics and Applications).
