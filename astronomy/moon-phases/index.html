Phases of the Moon

The Moon shines by reflected sunlight, so the Sun always lights one half of it. As the Moon goes round Earth, we see more or less of that lit half. That changing view is the phase. A full cycle, from new moon to new moon, takes about 29.5 days.

What is simplified

The model is schematic. The Moon is really about 384,000 km away, roughly 30 Earth widths, and the Sun is about 390 times farther still. Here everything is squeezed together so it fits on the table, which makes Earth's shadow look far wider than it is. Earth's own trip round the Sun is left out and the Sun is held still, so one turn of the slider is the 29.5-day cycle of phases, not the ~27.3 days the Moon takes to circle Earth measured against the stars. The orbit is drawn as a circle. The tilt of the orbit in step 3 is drawn about 4 times larger than the real ~5°. The "From Earth" window shows the Moon as seen from the northern hemisphere; from the southern hemisphere it appears the other way up. The faint glow on the Moon's dark part stands in for earthshine, sunlight reflected off Earth.
