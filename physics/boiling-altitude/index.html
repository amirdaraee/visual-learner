Boiling up high

Water boils at 100 °C at the beach but only about 70 °C on top of Everest. The reason is how hard the air presses on the water.

Boiling and altitude

A liquid boils when its vapour pressure reaches the pressure pressing on it. The air pressure falls as you climb, so the boiling point falls with it. A sealed lid raises the pressure, so the boiling point rises.

What is simplified

The scene is schematic: the molecules, the pot and the mountain are not to scale, and heating is sped up. Air pressure comes from the International Standard Atmosphere, a model of an average day; real pressure shifts with the weather by a few kPa. Vapour pressure uses the Buck equation, a fit that stays within a fraction of a percent of steam tables from 0 to ~120 °C. The water is pure, and effects such as dissolved salt, superheating and the small extra pressure from the depth of the water are left out.
